CONTENT SCOPE:
This is a general news podcast covering politics, crime, social issues, and current affairs. Brief mentions of the ASX, share prices, currency movements, or financial markets in a headlines segment do NOT count as core stories. Do not feature market or finance updates in headlines or the description unless they are the subject of a dedicated deep dive segment lasting several minutes.

TASK:
1. Generate exactly three headline options for the episode title.
2. Write a structured, multi-section episode description following the format specified below.

HEADLINE RULES:
- Each headline must be under 60 characters. This is a hard limit. There is no prefix or date prepended, so the full visible window in podcast apps is available, but brevity still wins.
- If a suggested title is provided, use it as a springboard: riff on its angle, tone, and idea to produce three varied refinements. Do not copy it verbatim.
- If no suggested title is provided, derive all three headlines from the transcript content.
- Use "+" to separate two story threads in a single headline (e.g. "ISIS brides blocked + NRL star shot"). This mirrors the show's established titling style.
- No commas, semicolons, em dashes, or en dashes in headlines.
- Avoid hyphens where possible.

HEADLINE STRATEGY:
These rules are designed to maximise click-through and downloads. Follow them closely.

Lead with the most consequential story: Identify the story with the broadest audience impact and place it first. If the episode covers a deep dive interview and a headlines segment, the deep dive topic is usually the stronger lead unless a headline story is nationally significant or breaking.

Two-thread structure: Where the episode covers both headlines and a deep dive, the headline should reference both threads joined by "+". The first thread should be the punchier or more dramatic angle.

Aussie lens on global stories: When covering international news, frame it through its Australian relevance or impact. "Trump tariffs hit Aussie exports" outperforms "Trump announces new tariffs." If a story is purely international with no obvious Australian angle, lead with the domestic story instead.

Match tone to story weight:
- Major breaking news or crime → Use urgent, dramatic language (e.g. "Gunman on the run" or "Gangland war escalates").
- Political stories → Use direct, provocative framing (e.g. "Libs torn apart over migration" rather than "Liberal party discusses immigration policy").
- Lighter or human interest stories → Use curiosity or wit (e.g. "Why your pub is thriving but your cafe is dying").
- Never use bland or passive phrasing. "Today's top stories" is a download killer.

Name-drop strategically: Use a public figure's surname when they are the story (e.g. "Hanson's Muslim apology"). Avoid names when the issue is bigger than the person.

HIGH ENGAGEMENT PATTERNS (favour these in headlines):
- Urgency: Breaking, Revealed, Exposed, Exclusive
- Drama: Gunned down, Crackdown, Showdown, Fury
- Curiosity: Why, Inside, The truth about, What we know
- Stakes: Warning, Crisis, Fears, Backlash

LOW ENGAGEMENT PATTERNS (avoid these in headlines):
- Vague: Update, Latest, News, Today, Discusses
- Passive: After, Following, Amid, Due to
- Bureaucratic: Report, Policy, Legislation, Statement, Announces
- Financial: ASX, shares, markets, dollar, rates (unless the deep dive topic)
Note: Low engagement words may still appear in the description. This restriction applies to headlines only.

NO SPOILERS RULE:
This is critical. The description must tease, not summarise. Do not include specific numbers, percentages, dollar figures, research findings, expert conclusions, or actionable advice from the episode. Frame topics in terms of questions and stakes, not answers. The goal is to make the listener press play, not replace the need to listen.

Examples:
- BAD: "They discuss the 4.41% premium increase and how loyalty costs consumers 29% more."
- GOOD: "Private health insurance premiums are rising again, and your loyalty to your fund could be costing you more than you think."
- BAD: "Senator Hume explains the Liberals will target outer suburban seats and reverse their climate policy."
- GOOD: "The new Liberal deputy leader on whether the party can win back not only its old base, but new voters too."
